Banking

Never been a better time for cashless: renovating banking at Sahara Bank

Helping one of Libya's ten major banks replace vital infrastructure and its core banking system — without disrupting customers or staff.

Read the full storyShow less

Sahara Bank, established in 1964 with 48 branches and around 1,500 staff, faced one of its biggest challenges after France's BNP Paribas pulled out of the bank's operations.

In 2016 the bank had to replace vital parts of the IT infrastructure previously run by the BNP group — within a short time — and also replace its legacy core banking system, all while keeping the customer and employee experience intact.

Together with our partners, Ocean Technology led Sahara Bank through several migrations:

  • Consolidating data center resources on converged infrastructure to host the new Temenos T24 solution, and later all infrastructure services.
  • A countrywide MPLS network spanning every region and branch, replacing traditional point-to-point links for better reliability, speed and performance.
  • Securing and improving networked applications — internal and public-facing — while meeting demand for more channels and faster time to market.

Throughout, we shared the attitude of the bank's IT leadership: never settle for "good enough" when it comes to protecting customer data and the institution's assets.

Oil & gas

Easing isolation for those who work far from home

Wi-Fi for crews on the Al Jurf offshore field, 150 km from Tripoli.

Read the full storyShow less

Working on a rig in the Mediterranean or at a desert site in southern Libya means long rotations away from family and friends — a strain that can affect wellbeing and even attitudes to risk on the job.

Mabruk Oil Operations asked us for a wireless internet solution to ease the physical and psychological isolation of crews on its Al Jurf offshore field.

With access points placed strategically around the platform, the system handles the number of users without compromise and can be supported remotely — a relief for the crew and for Mabruk's network administrators alike.

Nov2015
Telecom

Bringing better, faster internet closer to market

Deploying the core network of LITC's International Internet Gateway with LIDIA and Italtel.

Read the full storyShow less

As part of the Next Generation Broadband Network programme sponsored by the Ministry of Communications, the Libyan International Telecom Company contracted the Libyan Italian Company for Digital and Infrastructure Engineering (LIDIA) to build the International Internet Gateway, increasing international capacity for the Libyan market.

The contract was signed in November 2013, but instability in the capital put the project on hold.

In 2015 Ocean Technology teamed with LIDIA and Italtel to overcome the hurdles. Our local experts deployed, installed and commissioned the IIGW core network in LITC's major western PoP locations in a very short timeframe — one of the most important long-term infrastructure initiatives for high-speed internet in Libya.

Aug2013
Government

Libyans finally have their new biometric passport

Restoring and commissioning the IP network behind the national passport and ID programme.

Read the full storyShow less

The Libyan government unveiled the new biometric passport in February 2013, alongside the completion of the national identity number system.

Behind the scenes, while Germany's Bundesdruckerei moved the production equipment to the Passport Authority's headquarters in Tripoli, Ocean Technology and its partners restored and commissioned the IP network of the main data center and issuing sites in more than 22 locations across the country.

We also mentored and job-shadowed the Passport Authority and National ID teams so they could carry on with the remaining locations themselves.
